RESEARCH INTERESTS
My research interests lie at the intersection of mathematical theory, computational science, and data-driven
modelling, with a strong emphasis on addressing contemporary scientific and industrial challenges. In recent years,
my research has actively expanded towards integrating machine learning frameworks such as Physics-Informed
Neural Networks (PINNs) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) with classical governing equations to enhance
the analysis, prediction, and control of complex fluid flow and heat transfer problems. I am interested in hybrid
modelling approaches that combine first-principles mathematics with data-driven intelligence to overcome
limitations of purely analytical or numerical methods.
I also engage with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) as both a research and visualization tool, employing
platforms such as ANSYS Fluent to study flow behaviour, validate mathematical models, and generate high-
fidelity visual insights for complex transport phenomena. My research interests are in computational fluid
dynamics with applications to thermal systems and transient flow problems relevant to energy and industrial
engineering. Recently, I have conducted CFD investigations on shell-and-tube heat exchangers (STHX) to analyze
flow behavior, thermal performance, and design efficiency under realistic operating conditions. In addition, I have
studied sloshing dynamics in storage tanks, focusing on transient free-surface flows and their implications for
structural integrity and operational safety. These studies motivate my pursuit of postdoctoral research in CFD-

TEACHING STATEMENT
Philosophy and Context
My academic practice is grounded in the belief that rigorous mathematics, when communicated
with clarity and relevance, can significantly shape the intellectual confidence and problem-solving
abilities of engineering students in India. As a mathematician working at the interface of theory ,
computation, and application, I view teaching and research as mutually reinforcing activities.
Research keeps my teaching contemporary and inquiry-driven, while classroom engagement
constantly reshapes the questions I ask as a researcher .
Indian classrooms are uniquely diverse academically , linguistically , and socio-economically .
Teaching in such an ecosystem demands not only subject expertise, but also sensitivity , structure,
and adaptability . My approach to teaching mathematics and computation is therefore learner -
centric, scaffolded, and strongly aligned with the cognitive and professional needs of Indian
engineering students.
Writing as a Tool for  Conceptual Retention
One of the most powerful yet underutilized tools in mathematics education is structured writing. I
actively encourage students to write mathematics not merely solve problems. Through reflective
summaries, derivation notebooks, error-analysis reports, and concept-explan ation assignments,
students are guided to articulate why a method works, not just how it is applied.
Writing forces students to slow down their thinking, organize ideas, and confront gaps in
understanding. I have observed that students who regularly write short conce ptual explanations
demonstrate stronger long-term retentio n and transfer of knowledge, especially in courses such as
numerical methods, fluid mechanics, and mathematical modelling. This practice is particularly
effective in the Indian context, where examination-oriented learning often suppresses conceptual
articulation. Writing restores ownership of ideas and transforms passive learners into active
thinkers.
The Need for  Rigor ous and Structur ed Tutorials
In my teaching practice, tutorials are not treated as remedial sessions but as core learning spaces
for Gen Z learners. I design tutorials to be rigorous, interactive, and progressively challenging,
focusing on problem decomposition, estimation, algorithmic thinking, and interpretation of results.
Each tutorial is structured to bridge the gap between lecture material and real-world application.
For engineering students many of whom are transitioning from formula-driven school
mathematics, tutorials serve as the arena where confidence is built. I emphasiz e guided problem-
solving, peer discussion, and multiple-solution strategies. Rigorous tutorials cultivate
mathematical maturity , reduce fear of abstraction, and prepare students for open-ended engineering
problems rather than closed-form textbook questions.

Understanding Student Needs
A central element of my pedagogy is diagnosing student needs early and continuously. Through
diagnostic tests, classroom questioning , informal feedback, and reflective assignments, I assess
students’ preparedness, misconceptions, and learning barriers. This allows me to adapt pacing,
instructional methods, and assessment strategies in real time. Indian classrooms often contain
students with vastly different academic backgrounds. Recognizing this, I employ differentiated
instruction offering foundational reinforcement where needed, while simultaneously challenging
advanced learners through exploratory problems and mini-projects. Teaching, in my view, is
effective only when it is responsive.
I place strong emphasis on support ing both slow and fast learners with sensitivity and
confidentiality. Slow learners are assisted through one-to-one mentoring, scaffolded problem sets,
and confidence-building interventions without public labeling or academic stigma. Fast learners
are provided with enrichment tasks, research-oriented readings, and advanced problem challenges
in a discreet manner that preserves classroom equity and peer dynamics. This balanced approach
ensures inclusivity while nurturing academic excellence.
Curating and Pr opagating Quality Resour ces
In an era of information overload, the role of a teacher extends to that of a curator of quality
knowledge. I actively collect, evaluate, and disseminate high-quality learning resources, including
textbooks, lecture notes, open-source computational tools, simulations, and research-based articles.
Students are guided on how to read technical material, what to focus on, and how to connect
resources meaningfully .
By introducing students to structured repositories of learning rather than random  online searches, I
help them develop disciplined self-learning habits. Alongside traditional resources, I promote the
judicious and ethical use of AI-powered  learning tools such as ChatGPT , Gemini, and Perplexity .
These tools are introduced as cognitiv e aids for idea clarification, concept ual reinforcement,
exploratory questioning, and reference discovery—rather than as shortcuts for answers. Students
are trained to critically evaluate AI-generated content, verify results mathematic ally, and use these
platforms to enhance reasoning, not replace it.
A Differ entiated Appr oach
Teaching in India requires conscious differentiation between rural and urban student cohorts,
without compromising academic standa rds. Rural students often possess strong perseverance and
intuitive reasoning but may lack early exposure to computational tools, academic English, or
exploratory learning environments. For such students, I emphasize confidence-building, gradual
abstraction, language-sensitive explanations, and hands-on computation.
Urban students, while often more exposed to technology and competitive environments, may
exhibit gaps in conceptual depth and patience with rigor . With them, I emphasize disciplined
reasoning, derivations, proof-based thinking, and reflective learning. In both cases, the goal is
conver gence bringing all students to a common platform of mathematical confidence, autonomy ,
and professional readiness.
